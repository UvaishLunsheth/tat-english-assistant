PROSE
======================================================================
Source : pedagogy_1

DEFINITION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Prose includes fiction and non-fiction items, organized in paragraphs. It may or may not have all elements of fiction such as plot, character, theme, setting, point of view, style, and figures of speech. Prose teaching is primarily meant for language learning, enabling learners to develop reading comprehension, pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar skills.

CHARACTERISTICS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Prose includes fiction (novels, mystery, detective, romance, short stories, historical fiction, narratives) and non-fiction (essays, autobiographies, speeches, journals, articles).
2. Prose can be divided into types according to function: Narrative (story-telling in chronological order), Argumentative (persuades to believe something, examines facts/opinions to conclude), Descriptive (focuses on significant details of persons, places, processes, objects), and Informative (communicates information, seen in newspapers, reports, textbooks).
3. Prose is organized in paragraphs.
4. The important elements of prose (often elements of fiction) include plot, character, theme, setting, point of view, style, and figures of speech.
5. Figures of speech in prose can include Irony, Symbol, Simile, Metaphor, and Image.
6. Prose teaching aims to develop the ability to speak English correctly and fluently.
7. It involves both intensive reading (detailed understanding with specific aims) and extensive reading (reading longer texts for pleasure).

PRINCIPLES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Prose teaching is fundamentally for language learning, focusing on reading comprehension, pronunciation, vocabulary, content words, and grammar.
2. The objectives of teaching prose include understanding passages, reading fluently, enriching vocabulary, expressing ideas orally and in writing, enjoying reading and writing, and developing imagination.
3. Intensive reading is guided by specific learning aims and tasks, promoting detailed understanding, judgment, reasoning, interpretation, and appreciation.
4. Extensive reading encourages independent silent reading for pleasure, aiming to quickly understand subject matter, increase passive vocabulary, and develop a reading habit.
5. Teaching procedures, such as the Herbertian model, emphasize a structured approach starting from motivation and progressing through vocabulary, reading, comprehension, and evaluation.
6. The CCE method promotes a learner-centric approach with pre-reading, while-reading, and post-reading activities to enhance engagement, prediction, comprehension, and reflection.
7. Modern pedagogical approaches integrate technology like multimedia, online resources, and social networking to make ELT more engaging, provide authentic language exposure, and foster interactive learning.

SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Prose, a fundamental component of English Language Teaching (ELT), encompasses both fiction and non-fiction items, organized primarily in paragraphs. It is categorized by function into Narrative, Argumentative, Descriptive, and Informative types. Key elements of prose, often borrowed from fiction, include plot, character, theme, setting, point of view, style, and figures of speech like irony, symbol, simile, metaphor, and image. These elements are crucial for teachers to explain content effectively and provide literary insight.

The purpose of teaching prose at the school level is primarily language learning. It aims to develop learners' reading comprehension, correct pronunciation, stress, intonation, and articulation, enrich their active and passive vocabulary, enable them to express ideas orally and in writing, foster enjoyment in reading and writing, and stimulate imagination. Prose teaching involves two main approaches: Intensive Reading, which focuses on detailed understanding, specific learning aims, and tasks, helping learners to judge, reason, interpret, and appreciate texts; and Extensive Reading, which involves reading longer texts for pleasure, rapid comprehension, increasing passive vocabulary, and developing a habit of reading independently.

Historically, the Herbertian Model provided a structured procedure for teaching prose, including steps like Motivation/Testing Previous Knowledge, Declaration of the Topic, Teaching Vocabulary, Teaching Structures, Words for Pronunciation, Model Reading by the Teacher, Reading Aloud by Learners, Silent Reading, Comprehension Questions, Explanation of Content, Recapitulation, Evaluation, and Home Assignment. This model emphasizes a systematic progression through the lesson.

In contemporary ELT, the Continuous Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE) Method offers a more dynamic approach, dividing the teaching process into Pre-reading, While-reading, and Post-reading activities. Pre-reading activities aim to activate prior knowledge, predict content, and introduce vocabulary. While-reading focuses on comprehension, prediction, and information retrieval. Post-reading activities encourage summarization, reflection, critical thinking, and creative expression, fostering deeper engagement and understanding.

The integration of technology has significantly transformed prose teaching. Multimedia, combining text, audio, images, animation, and video, motivates learners, creates real-life contexts, enhances communicative ability, and breaks classroom monotony. However, its disadvantages include potential over-dependency, lack of teacher competency, and a risk of hindering interpersonal communication if not used judiciously. Online resources, such as websites, apps, and podcasts, provide vast, current, and accessible information, offering interactive games, authentic language exposure, and flexible learning opportunities. Similarly, social networking sites like Facebook, Twitter, and LinkedIn promote authentic, informal, and interactive learning, allowing shy learners to participate and facilitating quick information sharing among peers and teachers. These technological tools, when appropriately utilized, can make prose teaching more engaging, effective, and relevant to learners' lives, moving beyond traditional methods to create a more immersive language learning environment.

SHORT ANSWER QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Explain the difference between intensive and extensive reading in prose teaching.
Answer : Intensive reading involves learners reading in detail with specific learning aims and tasks, helping them to judge, reason, interpret, and appreciate a text. Extensive reading, on the other hand, involves reading longer texts for pleasure, often silently and independently, to quickly understand the subject matter and increase passive vocabulary.

2. List any three advantages of using multimedia in English Language Teaching.
Answer : Three advantages of using multimedia in ELT are: it motivates learners to learn English quickly and easily, it creates a real-life or native speaking country context for pronunciation, and it breaks the monotony and boredom of traditional class teaching, stimulating interactive learning.

3. Describe the main stages of the CCE method for teaching prose.
Answer : The CCE method for teaching prose involves three main stages: Pre-reading, While-reading, and Post-reading. Pre-reading activities prepare learners by activating prior knowledge and predicting content. While-reading activities focus on comprehending the text and finding information. Post-reading activities help learners summarize, reflect, and question what they have read, fostering deeper understanding and critical thinking.

LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Discuss the various elements of prose and explain how they assist a teacher in explaining content effectively.
Answer :
Prose is organized in paragraphs and incorporates several important elements, often borrowed from fiction, that collectively create a complete literary work. These elements include: Plot (the sequence of incidents or events), Character (a person or being in the narrative), Theme (the central idea or insight), Setting (the overall context of the story), Point of View (the perspective from which the story is told), Style (the author's manner of using words, constructing sentences, and handling tone), and Figures of Speech (words or phrases with non-literal meanings like irony, symbol, simile, metaphor, and image).

These elements assist a teacher in explaining content effectively by providing a framework for analysis and deeper understanding. For instance, understanding the plot helps learners follow the narrative flow, while analyzing characters allows them to grasp motivations and relationships. Identifying the theme helps learners extract the central message or moral. Discussing the setting provides context and atmosphere. Examining the point of view helps learners understand narrative perspective. Analyzing style and figures of speech enriches learners' appreciation of language use, literary devices, and the author's craft. By focusing on these elements, teachers can guide learners to interpret the text with literary insight, fostering critical thinking and a more profound engagement with the material.

2. Elaborate on the objectives of teaching prose and outline the Herbertian steps involved in teaching a prose lesson.
Answer :
The objectives of teaching English prose at the school level are multifaceted, aiming for comprehensive language development. These objectives include enabling learners to understand passages and grasp their meaning, read with correct pronunciation, stress, intonation, pause, and articulation, enrich their active and passive vocabulary, express ideas orally and in writing, enjoy reading and writing, and develop their imagination. Ultimately, prose teaching is meant for holistic language learning, encompassing reading comprehension, vocabulary, and grammar.

The Herbertian Model provides a structured, systematic procedure for teaching a prose lesson, typically involving the following steps:
1.  **Motivation/Testing Previous Knowledge:** The teacher asks questions related to the lesson or previous learning to elicit free responses and draw learners' attention.
2.  **Declaration of the Topic:** After motivation, the teacher elicits and writes the title of the lesson on the chalkboard.
3.  **Teaching Vocabulary:** The teacher selects active and passive vocabulary from the content and explains meanings situationally, using flashcards, real objects, or discourse.
4.  **Teaching Structures:** The teacher identifies grammatical structures and explains them using situations and substitution tables.
5.  **Words for Pronunciation:** Difficult words are selected, written on the chalkboard, pronounced loudly by the teacher, and repeated by learners until correct pronunciation is achieved.
6.  **Model Reading by the Teacher:** The teacher reads the selected paragraph loudly with proper pronunciation, stress, intonation, and pauses, while learners listen and try to imitate.
7.  **Second Reading by the Teacher:** The teacher reads the passage a second time for reinforcement.
8.  **Reading Aloud by the Learners:** Learners read the passage aloud with proper pronunciation, stress, intonation, and pause.
9.  **Silent Reading by the Learners:** Learners read the passage silently to comprehend the central idea or theme.
10. **Comprehension Questions by the Teacher:** The teacher asks questions to evaluate learners' comprehension of the passage.
11. **Explanation of the Content by the Teacher:** The teacher explains the content with suitable examples and writes a summary on the chalkboard.
12. **Recapitulation:** The teacher summarizes the entire lesson, including vocabulary, structures, and the main content, with the learners.
13. **Evaluation:** The teacher evaluates learners through relevant questions to determine if the set objectives have been achieved.
14. **Home Assignment:** The teacher gives a home assignment related to the covered content.
